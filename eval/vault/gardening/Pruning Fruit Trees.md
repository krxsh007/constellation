# Pruning Fruit Trees

Pruning shapes a fruit tree, lets in light and air, and balances growth against fruiting.

Basics:
- **Winter pruning** (dormant season) stimulates vigorous regrowth: use it to build the structure of apples and pears.
- **Summer pruning** slows growth and encourages fruit buds: used on trained forms (cordons, espaliers) and on stone fruit.
- Never winter-prune cherries, plums or apricots: silver leaf disease enters through the cuts. Prune them in summer.

Each year remove the three Ds (dead, diseased, damaged), then crossing branches, then aim for an open goblet shape. Don't remove more than ~20% of the canopy in a year or the tree responds with a forest of water shoots.

Cut just above an outward-facing bud at a slight angle. Clean, sharp secateurs; a pruning saw for anything thicker than a thumb.

The tree's vigour and size are set mostly by the rootstock it was grafted onto, not by pruning.
