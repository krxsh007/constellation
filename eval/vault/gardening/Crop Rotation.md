# Crop Rotation

Growing the same family in the same bed every year builds up its pests and diseases (clubroot in brassicas, blight and eelworm in potatoes) and drains the same nutrients. Rotation moves each plant family to a new bed each year.

My four-bed rotation:
1. **Legumes** (peas, beans): fix nitrogen, leaving it for the next crop.
2. **Brassicas** (cabbage, kale): heavy nitrogen feeders, so they follow the legumes.
3. **Roots** (carrots, beetroot, parsnips): don't want fresh manure.
4. **Potatoes and alliums**, then add compost before legumes come round again.

A three-year gap between the same family is the minimum for most soil-borne diseases; clubroot spores survive 20 years, so once you have it, you live with it.

Cover crops (clover, vetch, winter rye) between main crops keep the soil covered and add organic matter.
