# Forest Gardening

A forest garden imitates the structure of a young woodland edge with edible and useful plants in layers:

1. Canopy (standard fruit and nut trees)
2. Low tree layer (dwarf fruit trees)
3. Shrubs (currants, gooseberries)
4. Herbaceous perennials (comfrey, rhubarb, herbs)
5. Ground cover (strawberries, creeping thyme)
6. Root layer, and 7. climbers (grapes, kiwi).

Plants are grouped into **guilds**: a fruit tree surrounded by nitrogen fixers (clover, Siberian pea shrub), dynamic accumulators (comfrey), and insect-attracting flowers. It is companion planting extended to perennial, multi-layer systems.

Once established it needs little digging, weeding or watering: mulch, chop-and-drop comfrey, and harvest. Robert Hart pioneered it in the UK in the 1980s; Martin Crawford's garden at Dartington is the best-known example.

It is a garden, not a real forest; you still have to manage light, because the canopy will shade out everything below if left alone.
