# Companion Planting

Growing plants together that help each other. Some of it is folklore, but several effects are well supported:

- **Nitrogen sharing**: legumes fix nitrogen that neighbours can use; the classic case is the Three Sisters, where corn gives beans a pole to climb, beans feed nitrogen to the corn and squash, and squash leaves shade out weeds and keep the soil moist.
- **Pest confusion**: strong-smelling herbs and alliums mask the scent of carrots from carrot fly.
- **Trap crops**: nasturtiums draw aphids away from beans.
- **Beneficial insects**: flowers like calendula, phacelia and dill feed hoverflies and parasitic wasps that eat pests.

Poor companions compete for light or root space: e.g. fennel is allelopathic and stunts most neighbours.

This is the same thinking that forest gardens scale up into plant "guilds".
