# Grafting Fruit Trees

Fruit trees don't come true from seed, so every named variety (Bramley, Cox, Conference) is propagated by grafting: joining a cutting of the variety (the **scion**) onto a **rootstock**.

The rootstock controls the final size and vigour: for apples, M27 is a tiny patio tree, M26 and MM106 are mid-sized, M25 is a full-size standard. That matters as much as pruning for how big the tree gets.

Techniques:
- **Whip and tongue**: scion and rootstock of equal thickness, done in late winter with dormant scion wood.
- **Chip budding**: a single bud inserted in late summer.
- **Cleft grafting**: for top-working an older tree to a new variety.

The cambium layers (the thin green layer under the bark) must line up on at least one side. Bind with grafting tape, seal, and keep the scion wood dormant in the fridge until use.

A "family tree" has several varieties grafted onto one trunk, handy for pollination in a small garden.
