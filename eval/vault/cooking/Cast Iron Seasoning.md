# Cast Iron Seasoning

"Seasoning" a cast iron pan has nothing to do with salt and pepper: it is a layer of polymerised oil baked onto the iron. Heated past its smoke point, a thin film of fat oxidises and cross-links into a hard, slick, plastic-like coating that resists rust and food sticking.

How I season:
1. Scrub, dry completely on the stove.
2. Rub on a tiny amount of oil (flaxseed is hard but flaky; I use vegetable shortening or canola), then wipe it all off as if you made a mistake.
3. Bake upside down at 230-260 C for an hour. Repeat 3-6 times.

Maintenance: cook fatty things often, wash with a little soap (modern soap won't strip polymerised oil), dry on heat, wipe with oil.

Payoff: cast iron holds a huge amount of heat, which is exactly what you want for searing a steak, since the pan doesn't cool when the meat hits it.
