# Julius Caesar and the Rubicon

In January 49 BC Julius Caesar led the 13th Legion across the Rubicon, the river marking the border of Italy, which a governor was forbidden to cross with his army. "The die is cast." It meant civil war against Pompey and the Senate.

Background: Caesar had won election as consul in 59 BC with Pompey and Crassus (the First Triumvirate) and then spent nearly a decade conquering Gaul. The Senate demanded he give up his command before standing for a second consulship; without office he faced prosecution.

After defeating Pompey he was made dictator, eventually *dictator perpetuo* in 44 BC, and was assassinated on the Ides of March by senators led by Brutus and Cassius who claimed to be saving the Republic.

The irony: his death led to more civil war and to his heir Octavian becoming Augustus, the first emperor. The Republic's annual elections survived only as a formality.
