# Byzantine Empire

The Byzantine Empire is the modern name for the Eastern Roman Empire, ruled from Constantinople (founded by Constantine in 330 on the site of Greek Byzantium). Its people called themselves Romans (*Romaioi*) to the end. It outlived the Western Empire by almost 1000 years.

Highlights:
- **Justinian I** (527-565): reconquered Italy and North Africa, built the Hagia Sophia, codified Roman law (Corpus Juris Civilis).
- Greek replaced Latin as the language of government in the 7th century.
- Survived the Arab conquests behind the Theodosian Walls and with Greek fire.
- **Iconoclasm** controversies in the 8th-9th centuries.
- The 1054 schism with the Latin Church, and the disaster of the Fourth Crusade sacking Constantinople in 1204.

The empire's court was famous for elaborate intrigue, which is why "byzantine" came to mean needlessly complicated. It ended when the Ottomans took Constantinople in 1453.
