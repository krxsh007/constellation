# Partition of India

In August 1947 British India was partitioned into two independent dominions, India and Pakistan (with East Pakistan, later Bangladesh). The border, the Radcliffe Line, was drawn in five weeks by a lawyer who had never been to India, and published two days *after* independence.

The partition divided the provinces of Punjab and Bengal along religious lines. The result was one of the largest migrations in history: 10-20 million people crossed the new borders, and communal violence killed somewhere between 200,000 and 2 million.

Roots: the Muslim League under Jinnah demanded a separate state (the 1940 Lahore Resolution) as negotiations between Congress, the League and the British broke down after the Second World War. Mountbatten brought the date of British withdrawal forward to August 1947.

Legacy: the Kashmir conflict, three Indo-Pakistani wars, and divided families on both sides. It was the bitter end of the independence movement Gandhi had led.
