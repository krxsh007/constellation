# Athenian Democracy

Athens in the 5th-4th centuries BC was a direct democracy: adult male citizens (maybe 30,000 of a population of 250,000; no women, slaves or foreigners) voted in person.

Institutions:
- **Ekklesia** (Assembly): met about 40 times a year on the Pnyx; any citizen could speak; decided war, peace and laws.
- **Boule** (Council of 500): chosen **by lot**, 50 from each of the ten tribes; prepared the Assembly's agenda.
- **Dikasteria** (courts): huge juries of hundreds of citizens, also chosen by lot.
- Most offices were filled by lottery; only generals (*strategoi*) were elected, because competence mattered there.

Cleisthenes' reforms (508 BC) created the system, Pericles expanded it with pay for jury service. A distinctive safeguard was ostracism, the vote to exile an overly powerful citizen.

Critics like Plato and Thucydides blamed the democracy's volatility for disasters in the Peloponnesian War such as the Sicilian Expedition.
