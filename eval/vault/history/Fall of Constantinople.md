# Fall of Constantinople

On 29 May 1453 the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II captured Constantinople after a 53-day siege, ending the Byzantine Empire.

Why it fell this time when the walls had held for a millennium:
- The empire had shrunk to little more than the city; about 7,000 defenders faced perhaps 80,000 Ottoman troops.
- Mehmed brought enormous bronze cannon cast by the Hungarian engineer Orban, which battered the Theodosian Walls.
- He dragged ships overland on greased logs into the Golden Horn, bypassing the chain across its mouth.
- Promised Western help barely came.

The last emperor, Constantine XI Palaiologos, died fighting at the walls. Mehmed made the city his capital, and the Hagia Sophia became a mosque.

Consequences: Greek scholars fled west with manuscripts, feeding the Italian Renaissance, and the loss of the overland trade routes pushed Portugal and Spain to look for sea routes to Asia.
