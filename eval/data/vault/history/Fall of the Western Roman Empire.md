# Fall of the Western Roman Empire

Conventional end date: AD 476, when the Germanic general Odoacer deposed the boy emperor Romulus Augustulus and did not bother to appoint a successor. In reality it was a long decline through the 5th century.

Contributing causes historians argue about:
- Military pressure from migrating peoples (Goths, Vandals, Huns) and dependence on barbarian federate troops.
- Political instability: dozens of short-reigned emperors and civil wars.
- Fiscal crisis, debased coinage and loss of tax revenue from Africa after the Vandals took Carthage (439).
- The division of the empire: from 395 East and West had separate emperors, and the richer East survived.

Gibbon blamed Christianity and moral decay; modern historians (Peter Heather, Bryan Ward-Perkins) emphasise external shocks and economic collapse.

The Eastern half carried on for another thousand years as what we call the Byzantine Empire, which still called itself the Roman Empire.
