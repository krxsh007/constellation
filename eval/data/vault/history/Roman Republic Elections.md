# Roman Republic Elections

The Roman Republic (509-27 BC) elected its magistrates every year. The two **consuls** held supreme civil and military power for one year, each able to veto the other. Below them came praetors, aediles and quaestors, the *cursus honorum* ladder an ambitious man climbed in a fixed order with minimum ages.

Voting happened in assemblies, not by head count:
- The **Centuriate Assembly** elected consuls and praetors. Citizens voted in centuries weighted by wealth, and the richest centuries voted first, so the outcome was often decided before the poor voted at all.
- The **Tribal Assembly** elected lower offices and tribunes of the plebs.

Campaigning (*ambitio*) meant shaking hands in the Forum with a *nomenclator* whispering voters' names; bribery (*ambitus*) was illegal and constant.

The system broke down in the 1st century BC as generals like Marius, Sulla and Julius Caesar used their armies and popularity to override elections.
