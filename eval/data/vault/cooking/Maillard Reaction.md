# Maillard Reaction

The Maillard reaction is the browning reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars that creates hundreds of flavour compounds: the crust on bread, seared meat, roasted coffee, toasted nuts. It is not caramelisation, which is sugar alone at higher temperatures.

Conditions that matter:
- **Heat**: it gets going fast above ~140 C (285 F). Water boils at 100 C, so wet surfaces cannot brown until they dry out.
- **Dry surface**: pat meat dry, salt ahead of time, don't crowd the pan, or you steam instead of sear.
- **pH**: alkaline speeds it up. A pinch of baking soda helps onions brown and is why pretzels get so dark.
- **Time and sugar**: potatoes high in sugar brown faster (and can burn).

This is why searing steak in a ripping-hot pan and getting crispy, golden hash browns are really the same skill.
