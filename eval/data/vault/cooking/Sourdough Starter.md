# Sourdough Starter

A starter is a stable culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria living in flour and water. The yeast makes CO2 (the rise); the lactobacilli make lactic and acetic acid (the sour flavour) and lower the pH so unwanted microbes cannot compete. Same lacto-fermentation principle as kimchi or sauerkraut, just with flour.

My routine:
- Feed 1:1:1 (starter : flour : water by weight) every 24 h at room temperature, or weekly from the fridge.
- Use it at peak, when it has doubled and is domed, roughly 4-8 h after feeding.
- Float test is unreliable with high-rye starters; trust the doubling.

Troubleshooting: grey liquid on top ("hooch") means it is hungry. Pink or orange streaks mean bad bacteria, so throw it out.

A long cold retard of the shaped loaf overnight gives more sourness and a blistered crust, which browns deeply in the oven.
