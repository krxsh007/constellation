# Emulsions

An emulsion is droplets of one liquid suspended in another it doesn't mix with, usually fat in water. Without help they separate; an **emulsifier** has a water-loving end and a fat-loving end and coats the droplets.

Kitchen emulsifiers: lecithin in egg yolk, mustard, honey, garlic paste.

Examples:
- **Vinaigrette**: temporary; shake before use, mustard makes it last.
- **Mayonnaise**: oil whisked into yolk drop by drop; up to ~80% oil.
- **Hollandaise** and bearnaise: warm butter into yolks over gentle heat.
- **Beurre blanc**: butter whisked into a wine reduction.

Rescuing a broken emulsion: start again with a fresh yolk (or a spoon of water) in a clean bowl and slowly whisk the broken sauce into it. Heat is the enemy for egg-based ones: over ~70 C the yolk proteins scramble.
