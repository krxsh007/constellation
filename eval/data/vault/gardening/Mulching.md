# Mulching

A mulch is any layer spread over the soil surface. It suppresses weeds, keeps moisture in, buffers soil temperature and, if organic, feeds the soil as it breaks down.

Options:
- **Compost** or well-rotted manure: feeds the soil; 2-5 cm each autumn or spring.
- **Wood chip**: great for paths and around trees and shrubs, encourages fungal soil life. It does not "steal nitrogen" from plants when left on the surface; only when dug in.
- **Straw**: for strawberries and around vegetables, but can carry seeds.
- **Cardboard** under a compost layer to smother perennial weeds ("no-dig" beds, Charles Dowding style).
- Gravel or slate: decorative, no feeding.

Mulch onto moist, weed-free soil, keep it a few centimetres away from tree trunks and stems to avoid rot, and top it up as worms pull it down into the soil.
