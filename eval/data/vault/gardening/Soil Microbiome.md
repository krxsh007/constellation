# Soil Microbiome

A teaspoon of healthy soil holds billions of bacteria and metres of fungal hyphae. This soil food web does most of the work of feeding plants.

Key players:
- **Bacteria** decompose simple residues and cycle nutrients; some (rhizobia) live in root nodules of legumes and fix atmospheric nitrogen.
- **Mycorrhizal fungi** extend root systems by up to 100x, trading phosphorus and water for plant sugars. Most crops depend on them (brassicas are the exception).
- Protozoa and nematodes eat bacteria and release plant-available nitrogen.

How to look after it:
- Add organic matter: compost and mulch are food for the microbes.
- Minimise tillage, which shreds fungal networks.
- Keep living roots in the soil as long as possible (cover crops).
- Rotate crops to avoid build-up of host-specific pathogens.

Signs of good soil life: crumbly structure, earthworms, earthy smell (geosmin from actinomycetes).
