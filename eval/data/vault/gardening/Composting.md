# Composting

Compost is organic matter broken down by microbes, fungi and invertebrates into humus that feeds the soil food web.

Recipe for a hot pile:
- **Browns** (carbon): dry leaves, straw, cardboard.
- **Greens** (nitrogen): grass clippings, vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, manure.
- Aim for roughly 25-30:1 carbon to nitrogen by weight, about 3 parts browns to 1 part greens by volume.
- Keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge and turn it every week or two for oxygen.

A well-built pile reaches 55-65 C, hot enough to kill most weed seeds and pathogens, thanks to thermophilic bacteria. Finished in 2-3 months; a cold pile takes a year.

Smells like ammonia: too many greens. Not heating up: too dry or too much carbon. Finished compost spread an inch thick feeds the soil microbes and works well under a mulch layer.
