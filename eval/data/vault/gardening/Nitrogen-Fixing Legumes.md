# Nitrogen-Fixing Legumes

Plants can't use the nitrogen gas that makes up 78% of the air. Legumes (peas, beans, clover, vetch, lupins) host **rhizobia** bacteria in root nodules that convert N2 into ammonia the plant can use, in exchange for sugars. Healthy nodules are pink inside from leghaemoglobin.

In the garden:
- Grow them before hungry crops in a crop rotation (beans before brassicas).
- Cut the plants at the soil surface after harvest and leave the roots in, so the nodules break down in place.
- Clover or vetch as a cover crop or green manure adds up to 100-200 kg N/ha.
- In companion planting and forest-garden guilds, legumes are the nitrogen supply for the neighbours.

Inoculants: in soil where a legume has never grown, the right rhizobia strain may be missing, so seed can be dusted with an inoculant powder.

Too much nitrogen fertiliser actually suppresses fixation; the plant stops paying the bacteria.
